# Audience Matrix

Maps audience to pacing, vocabulary, and format. Treat these as starting
positions to adjust against real performance data, not as fixed truths about
people.

## Reading level

Default target is a Flesch-Kincaid grade level at or below 8.0 in the primary
layer, measured on the body copy excluding headings. When no scoring tool is
available, use these proxies:

- Sentences average under 20 words.
- Prefer the everyday word: "use" over "utilize", "start" over "initiate".
- One idea per sentence, one point per paragraph.
- Every acronym is expanded once, in plain words, on first use.

Raise the level only when the audience is explicitly specialist, and even then
keep the opening paragraph plain — experts skim the top too.

## Age bands

| Band | Pacing | Reference density | Caption style | Trust signal |
|------|--------|-------------------|---------------|--------------|
| Under 25 | Fast cuts, 1-2s per beat | High; current references land | Kinetic, on-beat, emoji-tolerant | Peer proof, visible authenticity |
| 25-40 | Medium, 2-4s per beat | Moderate; explain the reference | Clean sans, high contrast | Practical outcome, time saved |
| 40-60 | Steady, 3-5s per beat | Low; avoid in-jokes | Larger type, generous spacing | Credentials, track record |
| 60+ | Deliberate, 4-6s per beat | Minimal | Large type, strong contrast, no thin weights | Clarity, no pressure, easy exit |

Mixed-age content: write to the oldest band for clarity and to the youngest for
pacing. Clarity survives fast pacing; confusion does not survive slow pacing.

## Familiarity

| Level | Opening move | What to cut |
|-------|--------------|-------------|
| Cold (never heard of you) | State the problem in their words before naming your thing | Feature lists, internal terminology |
| Warm (follows, has not bought) | Show the specific outcome and what it costs | Re-explaining the basics |
| Hot (customer) | Lead with the new thing or the deeper use | The pitch |

## Offline versus online tone

**Online** content competes with a scroll. It earns attention in one second,
carries burned-in captions, and assumes the sound is off.

**Offline** content — print, in-room slides, handouts, signage — has a captive
reader and no algorithm. It can carry longer sentences, a full contact block, and
a QR code instead of a swipe-up. It cannot rely on motion, autoplay, or a link,
so the call to action must be executable on paper.

When producing both from one brief, keep the promise identical and change only
the delivery: the online cut compresses, the offline cut completes.

## Accessibility floor

Non-negotiable regardless of audience:

- Burned-in or platform captions on every video.
- Alt text on every image, describing content and function rather than
  restating the filename.
- Text contrast at or above 4.5:1 against its background.
- No information carried by color alone.
- On-screen text held long enough to read twice at a slow reading pace.
