Coalescing, with the stride in your hands

One wavefront, one instruction, the same arithmetic every time. The only thing you change is the distance between the addresses each lane asks for — and that alone decides how much bandwidth you get.

These are counts, not measurements. A 128-byte line and 4-byte elements make lane i touch line floor(i × stride / 32), and everything above follows from that. Real hardware also has caches, coalescers and outstanding-request limits, so measured bandwidth will not fall off as cleanly as 1/stride. A02's lab measures the real curve on your own GPU; a strided transpose there reaches about 15% of the copy ceiling, and staging through shared memory takes it to about 70%.
