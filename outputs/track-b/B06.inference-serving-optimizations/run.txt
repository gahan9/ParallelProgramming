Paging buys 4.2x the concurrency here, purely by not reserving for a maximum length that most requests never reach.
Paging's own waste is bounded: at most 15 unused tokens in the last block, i.e. 4.69 MiB per sequence (0.73% of a mean sequence). That bound is the whole argument for paging -- it converts an unbounded reservation waste into a waste you can name.

GQA shrinks the KV cache linearly in KV heads while leaving the query heads (and so most of the model quality) alone. This is why every recent open model ships with GQA.

KV quantization is the cheapest way to buy concurrency once GQA is already in place -- but it is an accuracy decision, not a free one (see B04). Measure task quality, not just perplexity, before shipping fp8 or int4 KV.

v rises as the batch fills: with enough concurrent sequences the decode step is no longer bandwidth-bound, the spare compute speculation was spending is gone, and the same draft model that helped at batch 1 now costs throughput. Speculative decoding is a *latency* optimization; measure it at your real concurrency, not at batch 1.

Read the sweep left to right: for a weak draft model (low alpha) the best k is small, because every extra draft token is paid for and rarely accepted. Raising k only helps while alpha^k stays meaningful -- the expected-token curve saturates at 1/(1-alpha) while the cost keeps growing linearly in k. That is the whole design rule.
