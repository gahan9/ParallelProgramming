3. When the answer is 'do not optimize'
  Sanity checks before you optimize anything
  - One GPU costs $2,336/month. No optimization can save more

  - Baseline hardware utilization is 70% of peak (70% spike

    right-size the fleet or revisit the headroom policy first.
  - The whole serving bill equals 18.3 engineer-weeks per
    month. If a project costs more engineer-weeks than that, it cannot pay
    back inside a year even if it makes serving free.

  monthly cost with cache         : $185,282
  monthly saving                  : $414,718

3. Why this works, and when it stops
  A cache holding a small slice of the key space captures a large slice of
  TRAFFIC, because traffic is skewed - that is the whole mechanism. Re-run
  with --zipf 0 for uniform traffic and watch the hit rate collapse to
  roughly cache_size / distinct_keys. Before you build a cache, measure the
  skew of your real request log; the exponent is the entire business case.

  What this model does not capture: staleness (how long a cached answer
  stays correct), personalisation and privacy (per-user keys destroy the
  skew, and a shared cache can leak one user's answer to another), and
  cold-start after every deploy. Those are the reasons real hit rates come
  in below the simulation, and they belong in the design review.
